Staff Mac & Cheese

Recipe

Overview

A practical, kitchen-friendly mac & cheese for staff food, built around what is usually lying around: cheddar or Westcombe for flavour, mozzarella for melt, cream cheese for stability, and a buttered herb breadcrumb top. This version is designed to run between 3pm and 5pm while you still need to do other jobs.

It is deliberately built to work with or without wine, and with or without proper stock. If there is no stock available, make a quick herb-and-butter "fake stock" and compensate by tasting more aggressively.

Ingredients (12 portions)

  • 1.2-1.5 kg dried pasta
  • 600-700 g grated cheddar and/or Westcombe
  • 300-400 g mozzarella, grated or torn small
  • 200 g cream cheese
  • 2-3 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 3 tbsp cornflour
  • 200-250 g breadcrumbs or panko
  • 150-200 g butter
  • Rosemary
  • Thyme
  • Black pepper or peppercorns
  • Salt
  • Olive oil
  • 250-300 ml white wine (optional)

Stock option

  • If stock is available: use about 2.5 litres chicken stock, simmered with rosemary, thyme, and peppercorns.
  • If no stock is available: use 2.5 litres water, extra salt, rosemary, thyme, peppercorns, and a small knob of butter to make a quick fake stock.

Timeline (3pm to 5pm)

  1. 15:00 - Start the base. Put stock, or your fake stock mixture, on to simmer with rosemary, thyme, and peppercorns. Let it tick for 20-25 minutes while you do other jobs.
  2. 15:05 - Start the breadcrumb mix. Melt the butter until foaming, add a little thyme and sage if available, then add the breadcrumbs. Toast lightly until aromatic and just starting to take colour. Do not fully brown them. Turn off and leave aside.
  3. 15:15 - Get pasta water on. Bring a large pan of salted water to a proper rolling boil.
  4. 15:20 - Reduce wine separately, if using. Put the white wine in a separate pan and reduce it hard by about two thirds. This concentrates the acidity instead of just diluting the base.
  5. 15:25 - Strain and reduce the base. Pass off the herbs and peppercorns, then reduce the liquid slightly so it has a bit of body. If you reduced wine separately, add it now and simmer briefly together.
  6. 15:30 - Cook the pasta. Boil until just underdone, then drain. Toss with a touch of olive oil so it does not weld itself into a lump while sitting.
  7. 15:40 - Stabilise the sauce. Mix the cornflour with a little cold water to make a slurry. Whisk it into the hot base and bring it just to a simmer until it thickens slightly.
  8. 15:45 - Add the cheeses. Add the grated cheddar/Westcombe and mozzarella in stages, stirring between each addition. Keep the heat moderate. Do not let it go into a fierce boil once the cheese is in.
  9. 15:50 - Make the cream cheese slurry. Put the cream cheese and Dijon into a bowl, add a ladle of hot sauce, and mix until smooth. Add this back to the pan in stages. The sauce should now be rich, glossy, and stable.
  10. 15:55 - Taste properly. This is the key moment. The sauce should be slightly too salty, slightly too rich, and looser than you think it should be. If it tastes flat, add a touch more salt, mustard, or a tiny splash of acid if available.
  11. 16:00 - Combine. Fold the pasta through the sauce. It should look almost too wet. That is correct, because the pasta and oven will tighten it up.
  12. 16:10 - Tray up. Put the mac into a gastro or baking dish and level it out.
  13. 16:15 - Top it. Scatter the breadcrumb mixture over the surface in an even layer.
  14. 16:20 - Bake. Bake at 180°C for about 20-30 minutes, until bubbling at the edges and golden on top.
  15. 16:45-16:55 - Final colour check. If it looks pale, give it a bit more heat or finish under stronger top heat if you have it.
  16. 17:00 - Serve. It should be creamy, molten, and spoonable, not stiff or claggy.

Key cues

  • Before the pasta goes in: the sauce should taste a little too strong and look a little too loose.
  • Before it goes in the oven: if it already looks perfectly thick, it will probably bake up too dry.
  • After baking: it should hold on a spoon but still slump back into itself.

Problems

  • Greasy or split sauce - cheese added too fast, or heat too aggressive once cheese was in
  • Dry mac - sauce was too tight before baking
  • Bland result - fake stock not seasoned assertively enough
  • Gluey texture - pasta overcooked before it hit the oven

Fix

  • For a split sauce, whisk in a splash of hot water and keep the heat low
  • For a bland sauce, correct with salt first, then Dijon, then acid if available
  • For a dry tray, next time leave the mixture visibly looser before baking
  • For a pale top, finish with hotter top heat rather than overbaking the whole tray
