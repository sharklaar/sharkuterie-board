Ingredients

  • 3 large onions
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • Good quality frozen peas
  • Milk
  • Water
  • Fresh mint
  • Vegetable oil
  • Salt
  • Optional: tiny splash of lemon juice or white wine vinegar

Prep

  1. Slice onions evenly.
  2. Roughly chop garlic.
  3. Pick mint leaves and keep them fresh for blending.
  4. Prepare an ice bath and cold tray before blending.
  5. Keep peas frozen until the last possible moment.

Cook / Plate

  1. Add onions to a large wide pan with a good amount of vegetable oil and a confident pinch of salt.
  2. Sweat gently for roughly 20–35 minutes until fully soft, collapsed and sweet-smelling, but not coloured.
  3. Add garlic and cook gently for 2–3 minutes only.
  4. Add equal parts milk and water, but do not make it too loose at this stage.
  5. Season again and bring slowly to a gentle boil, tasting as it heats.
  6. As soon as it reaches the boil, remove from the heat.
  7. Add frozen peas straight away and stir well.
  8. Cover and leave for around 3–5 minutes only. Do not leave for 10 minutes.
  9. Pass off the liquid, keeping the pea solids and liquid separate.
  10. For the first Thermo jug, add mostly peas, mint and just enough liquid to move properly.
  11. Blitz hard but briefly. Aim for a thick, vivid green pea purée first.
  12. For later Thermo jugs, loosen gradually with more reserved liquid until the full batch reaches the right consistency.
  13. Taste again for salt, pea sweetness and mint freshness.
  14. If it tastes good but slightly flat, add a tiny splash of lemon juice or white wine vinegar.
  15. Pass through a chinois.
  16. Pass into a cold tray over ice.
  17. Transfer straight to the fridge.

Timing / temperature cues

  • Onions should sound lazy, not aggressively frying.
  • Look for sweet onion aroma rather than raw onion or browning.
  • Do not hard-boil the milk and water for ages.
  • The frozen peas should crash the heat quickly after being added off the heat.
  • Keep the peas above 80C for as little time as possible to protect the colour.
  • Once peas are ready, blend and chill quickly.

Notes

  • Good peas matter. Cheap peas can be starchier, duller, paler and less sweet.
  • Salt the onions early: this helps draw out moisture and build sweetness, not just seasoning.
  • Frozen peas are already blanched, so the goal is reheating and extracting flavour, not properly cooking them.
  • Long heat exposure turns bright green pea soup duller and flatter.
  • The Thermo can introduce more air than a jug blender, which can make the soup look paler or foamy.
  • A concentrated first blend gives better colour and flavour than starting too loose.
  • Before passing, the soup should look slightly too thick and slightly too green.
  • Mint should go in late with the peas during blending, not cooked in the base.
  • If the soup tastes flat, check dilution before throwing loads more salt at it.
  • Cooling quickly preserves colour and freshness.
