```

Ingredients

  • Asparagus
  • Broccoli stalks / broccoli ends
  • Fresh spinach
  • Onions
  • Milk
  • Water or light vegetable stock
  • Vegetable oil
  • Salt
  • Small amount of parsley
  • Very small amount of mint (optional)
  • Lemon juice

Prep

  1. Prepare ice bath and cooling gastro before starting.
  2. Slice onions evenly.
  3. Chop asparagus into small pieces.
  4. Chop broccoli stalks.
  5. Wash spinach.
  6. Set aside an additional handful of spinach specifically for blanching and blending later.
  7. Prepare blender, chinois and storage gastro before cooking begins.

Timeline

  1. 10:00–10:15 — Gather ingredients, set up equipment, ice bath, Monster and nicotine administration.
  2. 10:15–10:30 — Sweat onions gently in oil with salt until completely soft but with no colour.
  3. 10:30–10:35 — Add hot liquid (roughly 70% water/stock, 30% milk). Season and bring to a simmer.
  4. 10:35–10:41 — Add asparagus and cook 5–6 minutes.
  5. 10:39–10:41 — Add broccoli for the final 2 minutes.
  6. 10:41 — Turn heat off. Add spinach to the pot and stir through residual heat for 30 seconds.
  7. 10:41–10:43 — Separately blanch reserved spinach for 10–15 seconds in boiling water, then shock immediately in ice water.
  8. 10:43–11:05 — Pass off liquor and begin blending.
  9. IMPORTANT: Start every blend TOO THICK. Use far less liquor than feels sensible. Create a vivid green purée first, then gradually loosen with reserved liquor until correct consistency is reached.
  10. Add the blanched spinach during blending for maximum green colour.
  11. Add a small amount of parsley during blending.
  12. If using mint, use only enough that nobody immediately identifies mint as a flavour component.
  13. 11:05–11:20 — Pass through chinois.
  14. 11:20–11:35 — Cool aggressively over ice bath, stirring occasionally.
  15. 11:35–11:50 — Final seasoning and consistency check.
  16. 11:50–12:00 — Fridge and clean down.

Cook / Blend Notes

  • Do not colour the onions.
  • Do not leave green vegetables simmering longer than necessary.
  • Keep spinach exposure to heat as short as possible.
  • The blanched spinach is primarily for colour enhancement.
  • Colour is won during blending and cooling, not during cooking.
  • Always adjust thickness gradually using reserved liquor.
  • A soup that looks slightly too green before passing is usually about right.

Taste Cues

  • Before blending, the vegetables should taste intensely green and slightly sweet.
  • If it tastes diluted before blending, reduce liquor usage before adding more salt.
  • If the finished soup tastes flat, first check dilution, then seasoning.
  • A tiny amount of lemon juice at the end can lift the soup without making it taste lemony.
  • Mint should be almost imperceptible if used at all. The soup should still read clearly as asparagus.
```
