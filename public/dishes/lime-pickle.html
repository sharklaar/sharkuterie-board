Ingredients

  • 8 unwaxed limes
  • 2 green peppers
  • 6–8 padrón peppers
  • 3 fresh jalapeños
  • 2 mixed green chillies
  • 1 whole bulb garlic
  • 80g fresh ginger
  • 2 tbsp black mustard seeds
  • 2 tsp nigella seeds
  • 2 tsp fennel seeds
  • 1 tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 2 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp sweet paprika
  • ½ tsp cayenne
  • 100ml mustard oil
  • 200ml vegetable oil
  • 25ml white wine vinegar
  • 35g fine sea salt

Prep

  1. Wash the limes thoroughly.
  2. Remove obvious pips only.
  3. Dice the limes (rind, flesh and juice) into roughly 8–10mm pieces.
  4. Dice the green peppers to a similar size.
  5. Slice the padrón peppers into chunky rings.
  6. Slice the jalapeños into medium rings.
  7. Slice one mixed chilli into rings and finely chop the other.
  8. Thinly slice the garlic.
  9. Julienne the ginger into fine matchsticks.
  10. Combine everything in a large bowl with the salt and mix thoroughly.
  11. Cover and refrigerate overnight.

Method

  1. The following day, taste the liquid released by the vegetables.
  2. If the liquor is pleasantly citrusy, keep it. If it is unpleasantly bitter or simply salty water, drain some or all of it.
  3. Heat the mustard oil and vegetable oil to approximately 80–90°C. Do not allow it to smoke.
  4. Remove from the heat.
  5. Add the black mustard seeds, nigella, fennel and fenugreek. Leave to infuse for around 3 minutes.
  6. Stir in the turmeric, paprika and cayenne.
  7. Pour the warm spiced oil over the vegetables.
  8. Add the white wine vinegar.
  9. Mix thoroughly until every piece is coated.
  10. Transfer to sterilised jars.
  11. Press everything beneath the surface of the oil. Top up with a little vegetable oil if necessary.
  12. Leave in a cool dark place for at least 2 weeks. Four weeks is the target before using in the curry.
