Ingredients

  • 2 skin-on duck breasts (about 250g total)
  • 1 large onion, finely diced
  • Garlic, finely chopped or grated
  • Fresh ginger, finely chopped or grated
  • A few coriander stalks, finely chopped
  • Scotch bonnet, finely chopped (optional — probably omit next time)
  • Ground coriander
  • Ground cumin
  • Kashmiri chilli powder
  • Turmeric
  • Cayenne, to taste
  • Homemade lime pickle
  • A little water
  • Chopped tomatoes
  • Pork stock
  • Salt

Method

  1. Put the lime pickle in a small pan with a little water. Cook on the lowest heat for about 2 hours, adding a splash more water if needed. The aim is to soften the lime rind and mellow the pickle without frying it.
  2. Score and season the duck breasts. Put them skin-side down in a cold pan and slowly bring up the heat. Render gently until the skin is deeply golden and crisp. Remove the duck while still undercooked and reserve the rendered fat.
  3. Cook the onion in some of the duck fat over medium-low heat. Take it very dark, sweet and jammy. Keep cooking for roughly an hour, adding small splashes of water whenever it begins to stick, scraping up the fond and allowing the water to evaporate before continuing.
  4. Add the garlic, ginger, coriander stalks and Scotch bonnet, if using. Cook until softened and aromatic.
  5. Add ground coriander, cumin, Kashmiri chilli, turmeric and cayenne. Continue cooking the masala slowly, repeatedly adding small amounts of water to prevent catching. Let each addition cook away before adding more. Keep going until the spices, onion and aromatics have become a deep, cohesive sauce.
  6. Add the long-cooked lime pickle and cook it into the masala for a few minutes.
  7. Add the chopped tomatoes and cook them down briefly.
  8. Add enough pork stock to loosen the curry to the desired consistency. Simmer and reduce until rich and glossy. Taste and correct the salt.
  9. Return the duck to the curry and cook only until the breasts reach the desired internal temperature. Rest briefly, slice and serve with the curry.

Turmeric & Cardamom Rice

  • 130g rice
  • 225g water
  • Turmeric
  • Star anise
  • Cardamom pods
  • Salt

Combine the rice, water, turmeric, star anise, cardamom and salt. Bring to the boil, reduce to a simmer and cover with a lid. Cook until the water is absorbed and the rice is tender.

Notes for Next Time

  • The very long onion cook is important: repeated water/deglaze cycles build sweetness and depth to balance the sour lime pickle.
  • Pre-cooking the pickle for about 2 hours softens otherwise tough lime rind and lets it integrate into the masala.
  • The pork stock adds a particularly strong savoury finish alongside the duck fat.
  • The Scotch bonnet worked, but the curry probably does not need it. Let the cayenne and pickle provide the heat next time.
